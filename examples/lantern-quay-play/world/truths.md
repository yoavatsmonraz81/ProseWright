These facts are hidden. Only you see them. They shape what characters do; they never appear in the direction to the narrator unless the POV character has learned them on the page.

1. Captain Varga is smuggling stolen Admiralty survey charts of the reef passages, rolled tight and packed in straw inside wine casks. He is selling them to a privateer's agent in Brest.
2. The forty casks are weighted with sand so they weigh what wine weighs at the weigh-house.
3. The manifest's wax seal was cut from a stolen chart and pressed onto the manifest so the weigh-house would wave it through. A clerk who knows seals can tell it is a survey seal, not a manifest seal.
4. Varga means to move the casks onto a cart before dawn and away inland.
5. Varga is not a monster. He is a merchant who has made a bad bet and owes dangerous people. He will try to buy anyone who notices before he threatens them, and he never confesses outright.

THE NIGHT (bottlenecks, in order; the scene cards pace them):
- Dusk on the quay: the Gull's Due comes in late; something about her cargo is wrong; Varga notices Wren noticing.
- Night in the harbour office: the forgery is proved by procedure, not intuition; Ilse puts the choice to Wren.
- Dawn in the lighthouse gallery: Varga comes to make Wren an offer while his men load the cart; the bell is within reach.
