LANTERN QUAY. A small walled harbour on a reef-bound coast, autumn 1792. The lamps along the quay are lit by hand each evening by a lamplighter with a ladder and a taper. A lighthouse stands on the mole, with a bell in its gallery: three hard strokes is the harbour's old signal for a ship in distress, and it wakes the customs house and the barracks along the mole. Arrivals are entered in the harbour ledger and weighed at the weigh-house before goods may leave the quay.

THE REEF PASSAGES. The coast is guarded by shoals. The safe ways through them are secret: only naval pilots may hold charts of the passages, which the Admiralty survey office at Kettering Point seals with a compass rose whose north point is broken. Carrying such a chart without warrant is a hanging matter. Privateers would pay a fortune for one.

THE HARBOUR OFFICE. A cramped room of lamp oil and damp wool where the clerk keeps the ledger. A high window looks out over the harbour; the lighthouse beam sweeps the glass every twelve seconds.

THE CALLOWAY SISTERS. Wren Calloway, nineteen, is apprentice to the harbour's chartmaker. Her older sister Ilse is clerk of the harbour ledger. Their parents are dead; the sisters keep rooms above the chandler's.

THE GULL'S DUE. A brig out of Lisbon and Brest, master Captain Amos Varga, a regular trader on this coast for years. She is never late.
