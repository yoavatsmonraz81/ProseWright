STORY CONTEXT — for COMPREHENSION ONLY. This grounds how to READ the scenes; it is NOT a list of beats and must NOT be treated as an answer key. Derive the beats strictly from the scene material you are given.
- SETTING: London, March 1888, late-Victorian period. The detective's rooms at 221B Baker Street.
- FORM: A first-person detective story narrated by Dr. John Watson, recounting a case brought to his friend Sherlock Holmes.
- HOLMES: a consulting detective who reasons from observed physical detail to certain conclusions. He treats emotion as a flaw in the reasoning instrument and refuses to theorize before he has data.
- WATSON: a medical doctor, recently married and returned to practice, who narrates the cases with warm admiration and stands in for the reader's astonishment.
- THE CLIENT: a masked nobleman who calls himself 'Count von Kramm' but is in fact a reigning king, come incognito on a matter he says could influence European history.
- THE MATTER: a compromising photograph held by a woman from the client's past, which threatens an impending royal marriage.
- HOW TO READ: the surface is a courteous consultation, but the real movements are in deduction, concealed identity, and the gap between what the client will admit and what is actually true. Read for shifts in who holds the advantage, what is revealed versus withheld, and the contrast between Holmes's cold method and the client's agitation.
